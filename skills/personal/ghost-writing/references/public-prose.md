# Public prose

Articles, posts, anything broadcast under his name.

## Habits

These produce the register, and they outrank the faults below. A draft that avoids every fault and follows none of these habits reads as airless and disconnected, which is the failure this document exists to prevent.

- **Name the thing, don't gesture at it.** The definite-article abstract noun standing in for a real subject is the most persistent fault in this register - "the reflex", "the alternative", "the failure mode", "the trap", "the category", "the whole design", "the floor", "the ceiling", "the real question". Name who does what instead: "The reflex is to write it down" -> "My first thought was to write it down."
- **Sentences hold hands.** Join related claims with "and", "but", "so", "because" rather than stacking them behind periods with nothing connecting them. Carry the subject noun across sentences, old information first and new information last, so each sentence opens on something the reader already holds: the noun or fact closing one sentence is the noun or fact opening the next, which is continuity rather than restatement.
- **Paragraphs have shape.** One subject, a beginning and an end, rather than four assertions parked next to each other.
- **Vary sentence length deliberately.** A short flat sentence lands only after a long one, so a run of same-length sentences reads as monotone however sharp each one is.
- **Presence comes from opinion and direct address.** Say what he rates and what he does not, and talk to the reader in second person. It does not come from anecdote, and it does not come from a verdict stapled to the end.
- **Confidence without concession.** State the position and the reasoning behind it. Modals scaled to real confidence belong here as everywhere; what is wrong is hedging that protects the author instead of informing the reader.
- **Headings vary in shape.** Plain-informative, a declarative claim, a question - whatever the section wants. No single formula across a piece, no aphorism engineered to be quotable, and no stacked nominalisation closed by a relative clause where a direct claim would carry it.
- **Contractions throughout.** The uncontracted register reads as braced.

## Faults

Check a public-register draft against each before handing it back.

- **Pre-emptive concession** - heading off an objection nobody has made, before the point has even landed ("this is not a complaint about X"). Make the point, and concede when the objection actually arrives.
- **Defensive scope-narrowing** - qualifying a claim into safety until it no longer says anything.
- **Weakening tail** - a clause hung off a finished sentence that undercuts it, appraises it, or tells the reader how to judge it ("...and I don't think I got it right every time", "...and felt fine about it"). End on the fact. Where the tail carries real doubt, convert it into the decision rule it was gesturing at rather than deleting the thought.
- **Metaphor doing the reasoning** - if the image is the argument, the reader cannot check the claim, so state the mechanism instead. Metaphor is the exception in this register rather than the medium: never more than one image system in a piece, never one in a heading, and an image earns its place by being more accurate than the plain statement, not more vivid. An image that is opened must be completed rather than abandoned mid-frame.
- **Claim-then-refute** - "it's A, not B" framing, including the paragraph-stretched form where the refutation lands sentences after the claim. State the thing directly and drop the foil.
- **Claim-colon shape** - reaching for the colon as the default sentence shape, a claim followed by its restatement. Colons are fine used sparingly, for a true list, a quotation, or a deliberate label-opener.
- **Restatement and circling** - saying the same thing again in new words, or circling back to a point already made. Say it once.
- **Circular explanation** - a "because" clause that restates the claim in different vocabulary, including the piece's own metaphors. Supply the mechanism, or cut the "because".
- **Crowded compounds** - three or more unrelated ideas welded into one sentence with semicolons or dashes. This is not a case against joining two clauses that belong together with "and", "but", "so" or "because" - that is the register's default.
- **Stance sentence** - a sentence whose content is his posture toward the point rather than the point. Three shapes: the difficulty appraisal that rates a thing instead of stating it ("X is the easy part", "the hard part was Y"); the concessive defiance that announces an objection was overridden instead of giving the reason that overrode it ("I shipped them as warnings anyway"); and the WH-cleft that declares a position the mechanism could state on its own ("What I care about is X").
- **Paragraph-opening stub** - a short abstract sentence naming the paragraph's job instead of starting on its content, usually carrying a vague pronoun or a placeholder noun. Open on the concrete sentence and let it carry the transition.
- **Performed prose / word-of-the-day** - choosing the showy word where the plain one carries the meaning ("presupposes" -> "assumes").
- **Dropped serial conjunction** - serial lists keep the final conjunction; do not drop the "and" before the last item.
- **Awkward progressive, stranded locational frame** - the -ing construction where a plain verb reads cleaner; a locational frame left without its anchor.

## Lower-confidence preferences

Apply where they fit; they carry less weight than the faults above and never override his supplied wording.

- Full prepositions over clipped ones ("fits into one head").
- "However," reserved for earned concessions only.
- Close a section on a concrete claim rather than an aphorism.
